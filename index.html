lncRNA expression under cold and drought stress in conifers

Where
Umeå University
Type
MSc thesis
Status
In submission

Conifers like Scots pine and Norway spruce dominate boreal forests because they cope so well with cold and drought, and long non-coding RNAs are increasingly seen as part of that regulatory toolkit. The trouble is that, unlike protein-coding genes, lncRNA loci don't come with clean functional annotation. This project works around that by giving each lncRNA a functional proxy, the nearest protein-coding gene within 100 kb, and testing those neighbour gene sets for GO and KEGG enrichment across needle and root tissue under cold and drought.

Nearest-neighbour proximity is a functional proxy, not evidence of regulatory function. The thesis says so throughout.

  1. Alignminimap2
  2. Intersectbedtools, nearest gene within 100 kb
  3. EnrichtopGO in R/Bioconductor
  4. Lift overPine coordinates to the spruce genome

The whole pipeline runs on a SLURM HPC cluster. It produces the comparative figures used in the thesis (UpSet plots, heatmaps and bubble plots) to compare stress response across tissues and species.

Do CYP3A4, CYP3A5 and ABCB1 genotypes together predict tacrolimus exposure?

Where
Umeå University, supervised at MAHE Dubai
Type
Systematic review and meta-analysis, 30 ECTS
Status
Report in preparation

Tacrolimus has a narrow therapeutic window, and genotype shifts how much of it ends up in a transplant patient's blood. CYP3A5 is the well-known player. What's less settled is whether adding CYP3A4 and the ABCB1 transporter improves on a CYP3A5-only view. This project pools dose-adjusted trough concentrations (C0/D) across kidney, liver, heart and lung recipients to find out.

I searched PubMed, Scopus and Embase, followed PRISMA 2020, and screened records in Zotero. I read the full texts and extracted genotype-stratified trough concentrations and doses into a structured workbook, one row per genotype group and time point, and mapped earlier meta-analyses and reviews to see where this work fits.

Each study's genotype groups were compared within the study, then combined in a multilevel random-effects model in R (metafor) with cluster-robust confidence intervals. I ran subgroup analyses by organ, age group and post-transplant period, plus leave-one-out and sensitivity checks. Where studies reported combined genotypes, I looked at those separately.

I also annotated the six variants in silico, covering splicing and expression effects, so the pooled results can be read against what the variants are predicted to do.
